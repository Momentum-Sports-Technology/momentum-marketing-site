# Plan: a home venue for Momentum Netball — Unit 28 North Way, Andover

Written 2026-09-09. Sources: Curchod & Co and Mileway brochures, Valuation Office rating list,
Momentum Netball's Xero (FY to 31 Aug 2025 and 2026), the 2025 "Netball Complex Revenue Model"
sheet, Test Valley Borough Council Sport Facilities Strategy (July 2020), England Netball indoor
club court design guidance, LTA padel construction guidance.

## 1. The short answer

Momentum Netball cannot take this lease on its own. The rent is 8 times the company's turnover and
the company has negative net assets, so no landlord will accept it as tenant without a guarantor.

Momentum can still end up playing in this building. The route is to be the **sport operating
partner and anchor tenant** inside a larger venue business that someone else capitalises, and to
use the things Momentum actually has: 500+ players, 40 matches a week of guaranteed evening
demand, England Netball affiliation, and a community-sport case that a planning authority and a
council freeholder will listen to.

Section 6 is the step-by-step plan. Section 7 is the go/no-go gates.

### Fit for netball

England Netball indoor club court guidance: principal playing area 30.5 m x 15.25 m, overall space
34.5 m x 18.25 m per court including run-offs, **clear height 7.5 m or more over the whole
overall space**. The eaves are 7.1 m. Whether the courts clear 7.5 m depends on the roof pitch and
where the courts sit relative to the ridge. This is unknown and needs a measured survey before
anything else. Momentum's current venues (Winton, Harroway, John Hanson school halls) are
typically 6.7 m to 7.5 m, so league play at 7.1 m is workable in practice. England Netball
regional or premier events would not be.

Indicative capacity on 4,047 sq m of warehouse, pending survey: 6 netball courts fill the floor
with nothing left over. A mixed layout of 4 netball courts (about 2,480 sq m) plus 4 to 6 padel
courts plus changing, café and reception is the realistic shape.

### Fit for padel

LTA guidance: 6 m minimum at the sides, 8 m clear at the ridge over the net. 7.1 m eaves with a
pitched roof should satisfy this. Padel is the sport that is filling warehouses across the UK in
2025 to 2026 and it pays daytime rent, which netball never will. Local supply: Pik Padel (3
indoor courts near Grateley), Andover Tennis Club (2 courts, opened July 2026), Padel People and
Bannatyne in Basingstoke, Winchester Racquets (3 courts).

## 5. Levers that change the numbers

1. **Charitable or CASC status for the venue entity.** 80% mandatory rates relief takes rates
   from £192,000 to £38,400. No revenue line in the model is worth as much. A CIC does not get
   mandatory relief; a registered charity or a Community Amateur Sports Club does. Momentum
   Netball Ltd can hold a management contract with the charity rather than own it.
2. **Landlord incentives.** The unit has been empty since 2023 and was refurbished at cost. With
   the new assessment effective 2 March 2026, the landlord's empty-rates relief (6 months for
   industrial) runs out around September 2026, after which they pay roughly £16,000 a month in
   rates on top of £37,500 a month of rent forgone. Ask for: 12 to 18 months rent free, a stepped
   rent, a capital contribution to fit-out, and a turnover-rent element. Ask whether they would
   split the unit; 11 loading doors make a physical split plausible.
3. **Test Valley Borough Council as freeholder and planning authority.** The ground lease may
   restrict use to employment classes; council consent is needed either way. The council's own
   2020 strategy says unmet sports hall demand is under 2 badminton courts today but forecasts a
   needs case for **an additional 6 to 8 court sports hall by 2036** (paragraph 2.3.32) and notes
   Andover Leisure Centre's netball hall is limited by means of escape and licensing (2.9.1). That
   is the paragraph to quote. TVBC also runs Andover Leisure Centre through Places Leisure, who
   could be an operating partner.
4. **The Army.** Army Headquarters is in Andover. Women's netball is a major Army sport. A defence
   community partnership supports the planning case, the daytime bookings and the Armed Forces
   Covenant Fund route.
5. **Grants.** Sport England Movement Fund is capped at £15,000 and is not a venue-scale source.
   England Netball facilities funding, Sport England strategic facility investment via the council,
   the Football Foundation multi-sport programme, and a community share offer (sports clubs have
   raised £500,000 to £1,000,000 this way) are the realistic capital sources for a charity-led
   venue.
6. **Mini Momentum.** Holiday camps, school daytime hire and after-school programmes are the
   daytime fill that a netball league cannot provide.

## 6. The plan

### Phase 0 — decide the vehicle (this month)

- Momentum Netball Ltd does not sign anything. Form or identify a **venue entity**: a new
  company with an investor or padel operator holding the head lease, or a charity / CASC that
  Momentum helps found. Momentum takes a sub-lease or licence on 2 to 4 courts for evenings and
  weekends, plus a management or coaching contract for the netball programme.
- Agree with the co-directors what Momentum is putting in: the player base, the league
  operation, the brand, staff time, and no cash.

### Phase 1 — kill the unknowns (4 to 6 weeks, cost a few thousand pounds)

1. **Measured survey**: clear height across the floor against the 7.5 m netball line and the
   6 m / 8 m padel lines, column grid, floor flatness and loading, heating and ventilation for
   occupied sport, means of escape for 250+ people at peak.
2. **Planning pre-application with TVBC**: change of use B8 to E(d), loss of employment
   floorspace policy, parking and traffic at 21:00 on a business park, noise. Ask at the same time
   whether the ground lease user clause allows leisure. A "no" here ends this building and moves
   the search.
3. **Landlord call** (Keith Enters, Curchod & Co, 07803 411940; Mileway South East): how long
   vacant, appetite to split, rent free, stepped rent, capital contribution, turnover rent,
   what covenant or deposit they need. Do not disclose Momentum's accounts; the tenant will be
   the venue entity.
4. **Rebuild the 2025 model** with rent, rates (with and without 80% relief), FRI, utilities
   net of PV, staff, and a padel or multi-sport revenue block. Netball utilisation only in
   evenings and weekends. Momentum's own bookings entered as a contracted sub-lease.

### Phase 2 — find the money (2 to 3 months, in parallel with phase 1)

- **Padel operators** who want a South-of-England warehouse: Pik Padel (local, may want to
  scale), Padel People, Game4Padel, Rocket Padel, Padium, Punk Padel. Pitch: a 47,000 sq ft
  refurbished A+ box next to the A303, a landlord under pressure, and a ready-made community and
  netball anchor that de-risks planning.
- **Places Leisure / TVBC**: a council-supported community sports hall that answers the 2036
  needs case in its own strategy.
- **Community share offer** plus England Netball and Sport England capital, if the charity
  route is chosen.
- Order-of-magnitude capital need for a full fit-out (estimates, not costed): sports floor
  £270,000 to £420,000, 6 to 8 indoor padel courts £280,000 to £400,000, heating and ventilation
  £100,000 to £200,000, changing, café, reception £150,000 to £300,000, fees and contingency
  15%, rent deposit or guarantee 6 to 12 months £225,000 to £450,000. **£1.2m to £2.0m.**

### Phase 3 — heads of terms (only after phases 1 and 2 clear)

- Venue entity signs heads of terms with the landlord. Momentum signs a sub-lease or licence
  with the venue entity for fixed court blocks, at a rate no higher than its current £14,647 a
  year for the same hours, rising with volume.
- Planning application submitted with Momentum, the Army and TVBC leisure as supporting parties.

### Phase 4 — fit-out and open

- Momentum moves all Andover leagues in on opening. Mixed league, Netball Hour, pay to play,
  walking netball and Mini Momentum camps fill the programme. The "New Centre Opening" line
  already on the website becomes true.

### What the model says

1. **Base case as a CIC with no rates relief is break-even before loan repayments** and loses
   about £94,000 a year after them. Years 1 and 2 need roughly £90,000 of working capital
   even with 12 months rent free.
2. **Rates relief is the swing.** Charity status (80% mandatory) or TVBC discretionary relief
   (up to 100% for a local non-profit recreation body under policy v2026.1) is worth £154,000
   to £192,000 a year. No single income line is worth as much.
3. **Padel and the café carry the daytime.** Netball, gym and events together are about half
   the income. A netball-only or netball-plus-gym venue does not cover the rent.
4. **The Low case is a £550,000 a year loss.** If fill rates come in a third below Base
   across the board, the rent does not flex and the venue fails. That is the risk to price
   into the lease negotiation: rent free, stepped rent, a break clause and a turnover rent.
5. **Capital need is about £1.7m to £1.8m** including a six-month deposit and working
   capital. The Capital sheet carries a target funding stack of £1.25m grants, donations and
   equity plus £600,000 of loans. Every figure is a target from published programme ranges,
   not a commitment.

### If we created a CIC

A CIC limited by guarantee is the right vehicle to move fast: weeks to register, directors can
be paid, and it opens the Lottery, Football Foundation, LTA, Pitch Together and Charity Bank
routes. It costs two things:

- No mandatory 80% rates relief. TVBC can award up to 100% discretionary relief to a local
  non-profit recreation body, but the council bears 40% of the cost itself, the policy asks
  for two years' signed accounts, and it is a yearly decision, not a right.
- No Gift Aid, and no access to the charity-only trusts.

Recommendation: register the CIC now and apply to TVBC for discretionary relief on day one.
Found a charitable arm (a CIO) in parallel so donations, Gift Aid and trust grants have a home,
or plan to move the venue-holding entity to a CIO once trading. The Structure sheet compares
all four options. VAT needs an accountant before anything is signed: a VAT-exempt sports body
cannot recover VAT on £1.4m of fit-out or on the rent.

### Momentum Netball Ltd renting courts from the CIC

This is the cleanest structure and the model now runs on it by default (Inputs, mode 2).
Momentum Netball Ltd stays the directors' business with no asset lock. The CIC is the venue,
holds the lease, attracts the grants and the rates relief, and sells Momentum a block of
evening court-hours at its published rate less the block-booking discount available to any
block booker. For the CIC it is neutral to slightly positive against running the programmes
itself: it loses the programme income and the coaching cost together and gains about £49,000
of anchor court hire.

The catch is on Momentum's side. School halls cost Momentum about £24 a court-hour today. At
an arm's-length £41 (£48 less 15%) the current £18 per team per match no longer covers the
court:

| | Today | In the CIC, same fees | In the CIC, £25 per team per match |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| League income | 32,400 | 64,800 | 90,000 |
| Court hire | (14,400) | (48,960) | (48,960) |
| Other direct costs | (5,400) | (10,800) | (10,800) |
| League gross profit | 12,600 | 5,040 | 30,240 |

Commercial leagues charge £56 to £70 per team per match. A move from £18 to £25 is a
£400 to £560 season sub, still the cheapest in the market, and it is what makes Momentum
Ltd better off in the new venue than it is today. Governance: shared directors declare the
conflict, the CIC board gets independent members, and the court-hire contract is at the
published rate.

### What it does to the layout

- **A club-standard court fits one per span.** Overall space 18.25 m in a 20 m span leaves about
  0.9 m each side to the column faces. Courts run north to south along the span. Three spans
  take a full 34.5 m court; the east block is 33.5 m deep above the offices, so a fourth court
  needs 1.5 m end run-offs or the office strip removed.
- **Padel does not fit the 20 m grid.** A padel court is 20 m long and needs a clear 20 m plus
  access. It fits only in the 21 m spans of the extension and east block, running east to west.
  Two courts sit in the extension south of Court 3 and three in the east block, if the 75 m line
  is clear. Otherwise padel needs a column removed and a transfer beam, roughly £20,000 to
  £40,000 per column.
- **Span ends are 18 m × 20 m.** Gym in one, two pickleball courts or a junior and walking netball
  court in the other. Pickleball needs 18.3 m × 9.1 m per court and about 5.5 m of height.
- **Height.** Eaves and valleys are 7.1 m at every column row; each span has its own ridge. At a
  6° pitch the ridge is about 8.1 m and the court sidelines about 7.2 m. Below England Netball's
  7.5 m at the edges, fine for league play. The pitch is not on the plan. One laser measurement at
  a column and one at a ridge gives the profile.

Net effect on the model: 3 netball plus 5 padel instead of 4 plus 4. Netball external hire drops
by about a quarter and Momentum's evening block uses half the remaining evening capacity;
one more padel court adds about £85,000. Roughly revenue-neutral, and the padel side now
depends on the 75 m line.
